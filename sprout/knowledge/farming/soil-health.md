# Soil Health Basics

Healthy soil gives better yields, holds more water and needs fewer inputs over time.

## Test your soil

Take samples from several spots in each field, mix them and send them to a lab at least once a year. The test shows pH, organic matter and the main nutrients (nitrogen, phosphorus and potassium). Fertilise according to the results instead of guessing. Soil test reports are also needed for the Organic Farming Transition Program.

## Soil pH

Most crops grow best between pH 6.0 and 7.0. Acidic soils can be improved with lime, and alkaline soils with organic matter and sulphur-based amendments.

## Build organic matter

- Add compost or well-rotted manure.
- Keep the soil covered with mulch or cover crops.
- Leave crop residues to decompose when they are disease-free.
- Use green manure crops such as sunhemp or legumes, which add nitrogen.

## Reduce damage

- Avoid over-tilling, which breaks soil structure and loses carbon.
- Rotate crops, including legumes, to improve fertility and break pest cycles.
- Avoid driving heavy machinery on wet soil because it causes compaction.

## Signs of unhealthy soil

Water pooling or crusting on the surface, hard compacted layers, pale or stunted plants, and few earthworms all indicate that the soil needs more organic matter and better management.
