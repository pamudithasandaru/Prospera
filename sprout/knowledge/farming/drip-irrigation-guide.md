# Drip Irrigation Guide

Drip irrigation delivers water slowly to the root zone of each plant through tubes and emitters. It typically uses much less water than flood irrigation and reduces weeds and leaf disease because the foliage stays dry.

## When it works well

Drip suits row crops, vegetables, fruit trees and plantation crops, especially where water is limited or expensive.

## Main components

- Water source and pump (including solar pumps)
- Filter, to stop emitters from clogging
- Main line and sub-main pipes
- Lateral lines with emitters or drippers
- Pressure regulator and valves
- Fertiliser injector for fertigation

## Design tips

- Match emitter spacing to the crop: closer for vegetables, wider for trees.
- Keep laterals short enough that pressure stays even along the line.
- Group crops with similar water needs into the same zone.
- Use a screen or disc filter, and flush the lines regularly.

## Maintenance

Check for leaks and blocked emitters every week. Clean filters, flush lines at the end of each cycle, and protect pipes from sunlight and rodents.
